# Ticket-to-cash audit: the one-page format

Sent free to every supplier who shares an export, within three days of receiving it. It is the first demonstration of the service and the hook for the pilot ask. One page. Numbers from `02-ticket-aging-export.md`; no opinions beyond the last section.

---

**[Supplier name]: where your ticket days go**
Prepared by Quiet Bands, [date]. From your export of [N] tickets, [date range].

**Headline.** From ticket to cash you wait a median of [X] days. [Y] of those days are inside your customers' approval step, which is the part that can be chased. $[Z] is sitting there right now.

**What this costs you**
Dollars awaiting approval × your factoring rate or cost of capital × days / 365 = $[ ] per month, before counting the hours your office spends in [N] portals.

**What we would do about it, for two weeks, free**
Work your open queue daily under your logins and written authorisation: chase signatures with ticket images, fix preventable disputes before they happen, push approvals on each operator's own escalation path, and send you this table every Friday. You decide every price, scope and term question; we never touch those. At the end of two weeks you see the before-and-after on stage C and decide whether it is worth anything.

---

## Rules for writing it

- If stage D holds the days, say so plainly and do not make the pilot offer. "Your delay is payment terms; chasing will not move it; factoring or renegotiating terms will." That honesty is worth more than a pilot that cannot work, and the supplier will remember who told them.
- If stage A holds the days, say that the fix is in the field, offer one idea (a signature-at-completion rule, a photo-of-signed-ticket text from the crew), and still offer the pilot only if stage C is also material.
- Never name other suppliers or quote another supplier's numbers.
- Send it as a PDF and paste the headline into the email body. Owners read the email; controllers open the PDF.
